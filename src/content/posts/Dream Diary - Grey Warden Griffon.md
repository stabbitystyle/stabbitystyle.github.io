Had a dream about a Grey Warden deciding to become a griffon and leave humanity behind.  It really rubbed me the wrong way and I've been trying to parse out my feelings about it.  I think there's just something about a rejection of the amazing things about being human?  The art, the culture, and the raw intelligence to appreciate those things and create those things.  Or the technology and research and architecture and ability to change the world around them and improve their lives with things like medicine and shelter.  Or even basic things like permanent shelter or cooked food.  In the dream, she was an accomplish, relatively high ranking, relatively respected member of the Grey Wardens.  And I guess had earned access to magic that basically let them do whatever to themselves.  And there was the gist of a conversation with her about how she was going to try it out and if she liked it, would essentially commit ego death and lose herself in it.  I've also thought about like, the circumstances surrounding it in that setting.  Like, living a life as a Grey Warden just kinda sucks in general, they really don't live like, that far above a beast.  They spend lots of time in the wilderness, eat crappy military food, don't really have the time to enjoy most of the great things that humanity has to offer.  Basically they have a life of killing until they die a horrible death since there's no retirement from being a Grey Warden.  But I also thought about just transplanting that ego death into feral animal idea to modern day and it just doesn't hit the same as it did in my dream.  I'm not sure if it being a dream is specifically what made it hit and get stuck in my mind so changing it loses that connection?  I think there's also something about it as ego death vs actual death.  Like, one of the appealing things about actual death is that it's the end.  Your burdens are over, you don't have to struggle to live and sustain yourself, your pains and worries and anxieties and suffering are gone.  But with this feral ego death, you still have to worry about all of that.  If anything they become harder.  No medicine, no safe shelter, no agriculture so no safe steady food source, etc.  Honestly, weirdly enough, I've been thinking about my reaction to if that character had committed suicide instead and the reaction I feel is much less visceral.  I'm not really sure why, perhaps because that's like, a real phenomenon so I'm more used to it compared to something like this that is uniquely fantastical and thus novel.

I also ended up thinking about what I'd use that sort of magical power of self change to transform into whatever I desired and my thoughts returned to a gender transformation, which has become a recurring theme for me.  Still not sure my full feelings on that.  I'm sure there's some trans egginess there, but also I have so many like, weird repressive thoughts about gender and sexuality and attractiveness and my own negative views of myself and attractiveness and self worth and self image that I don't know if that's really like, a desire to be a woman or just a desire to have a form that I find sexually attractive myself.  I think there's also some like, inherent rejection of the idea because any actual path to that in real life would probably end with something I doubt I'd ever be happy with.  I don't think it'd ever be enough, that I'd reach a point and feel like I'm truly in the body I want to be in.  Which I suppose is what body acceptance and body positivity is about.  But I'm just so revolted by my body as it is that I'm not really sure I believe those on subconscious level, I just intellectually understand them and support them.  I know I need to diet and exercise if I ever want to progress towards something I'd be more happy with, but I don't know.  Food is like, one of the few things that really gives me deep satisfaction even through the managed depression.  I've tried moderating a bit but it never really sticks because I always end up craving that satisfaction that it can provide.  I guess that's probably something akin to food addiction?  I did keto for like 2 years or something and lost like 100+ pounds and was fairly close to a healthy body I could be comfortable in, but it was such a miserable relationship to food.  Pretty much everything I truly enjoy was locked from me.  Pastas, sandwiches, rice, sweets, sodas, etc.  All stuff that's bad for me, but which are so deeply, primally satisfying and comforting that there was just a permanent feeling of lacking.  And once I broke and had a bit of it, it basically all came crashing down since if I could excuse eating that stuff as a rare treat, it became impossible to actually manage myself and just became what I ate regularly.  Which probably cycles back into the thoughts on food addiction, with the relapse mentality.

I suppose this veered kinda off my original topic, but it's like 2:30am and this is basically just me trying to process that shit before my brain wants to go back to sleep again.  Fuck I'm gonna be tired at work today, I really don't want that.  Definitely have thoughts I want to write and work through about just how miserable I feel about work, but I think I'll save that for another time.
